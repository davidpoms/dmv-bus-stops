# DMV Bus Stop Intelligence Project Handbook

DMV Bus Stop Intelligence treats a bus stop as part of the transit experience,
not merely a sign where a vehicle arrives. A trustworthy record of waiting
conditions can help communities ask better questions about comfort,
accessibility, maintenance, and public investment.

The project follows four principles:

1. **Evidence before conclusions.** Preserve the source, date, identity match,
   uncertainty, and disagreement behind a claim.
2. **Presence is not quality.** A bench or shelter can exist while still offering
   a limited waiting experience.
3. **Prioritization is not approval.** A ranking can organize review work; it
   cannot establish engineering feasibility or require construction.
4. **Community history has value.** Dated observations should accumulate rather
   than overwrite one another.

The current product represents every active stop in a broad seating-improvement
opportunity universe and directs reviewers toward useful evidence questions.
This replaces the handbook’s older framing of a narrow “where should a new bench
be installed first?” product. A separate, narrow bench-candidate dataset still
exists for qualified planning analysis, but it is not the public opportunity
universe.
