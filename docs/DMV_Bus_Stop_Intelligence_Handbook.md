# Help improve bus stop information across the DMV

DMV Bus Stops is a community project that builds a clearer picture of bus-stop
waiting conditions in Washington, DC, Maryland, and Virginia. Public transit
data can tell us where a bus serves. It often cannot tell us whether a rider has
a bench, useful shelter, a clear waiting path, or a comfortable place to wait.

Volunteer observations help fill those gaps.

## Why this work matters

A stop is more than a point on a map. Its waiting conditions affect comfort,
dignity, and access to transit. Conditions also change: seating can be added or
removed, construction can alter a waiting area, and online imagery can become
out of date.

Better evidence can help communities understand where more investigation or
attention may be useful. It does not decide where an agency must build or what
work is technically possible.

## Who can volunteer

Anyone who can make a careful observation can help. You do not need transit,
planning, engineering, or accessibility expertise. You can review stops you
ride, stops near you, or stops selected by the site.

You may review anonymously. If email sign-in is available, it can keep your
review history together across devices. Email is not displayed with public
observations.

## What volunteers do

A review asks about visible waiting conditions, such as:

- whether a bench or shelter is visible;
- what kind of seating is present and whether it has apparent limitations;
- how exposed the waiting area appears to weather;
- whether the visible path or waiting space appears clear or constrained; and
- in person, how busy the stop appears and whether riders avoid its facilities.

One review can be completed at a time. Take the time you need, and use
**Unsure** whenever the evidence is not clear.

## Observation, suggestion, and uncertainty

The project keeps three ideas separate:

| Kind of information | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Volunteer observation | What a person reported seeing at a particular time, in person or in dated imagery |
| Evidence suggests | Available sources point toward a condition, but do not fully confirm it |
| Not enough information | The project does not have reliable evidence for a conclusion |

Some sources may also disagree. An inventory entry, map feature, or old image is
not the same as a current observation. Absence of evidence is not evidence that
an amenity is absent.

## What happens after a review

Your submission becomes a dated part of the stop's observation history. It does
not replace earlier observations. As careful observations accumulate, they can
strengthen or challenge what the project currently suggests and help identify
what should be checked next.

Nearby boarding locations can have different benches, shelters, or travel
directions. The project keeps those stops distinct when the transit data does.
If a stop identity is retired, its page points to its current successor stop or
stops so the history remains understandable.

## What a review cannot decide

A visual review does not establish:

- engineering or construction feasibility;
- accessibility or ADA compliance;
- property ownership or permission;
- permitting approval;
- underground or overhead utility clearance; or
- whether an agency should install an improvement.

Route-based rider exposure shown by the site is also not an observed count of
people boarding at that stop. It is context used to order review work, not a
condition a volunteer must verify.

## Participate safely and respectfully

- Observe only from a safe, lawful public location.
- Do not enter private property, stand in traffic, or obstruct riders or transit
  operations.
- Do not identify riders or include sensitive personal information in notes.
- The site does not ask you to upload photos. Do not take unnecessary photos of
  identifiable people.
- Skip a stop or choose **Unsure** when a safe, reliable observation is not
  possible.

## Get started

From the dashboard, choose **Review a seating opportunity**, **My Routes**,
**Near Me**, or a stop from the map. Before answering, confirm the stop name,
location, and serving direction shown on the review page. The
[Volunteer Review Handbook](/volunteer-handbook) walks through the
survey step by step.

Questions or concerns should go to the support contact displayed by the site or
to your pilot coordinator.
